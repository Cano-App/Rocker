Settings

Roughly the height of the navel when seated in the chair on its rockers. Set to 25.5 in, the published figure for a normal-height rocker, on the basis that the cushion lifts you to about that height.
Where the rocker touches the floor with you seated and relaxed, as a share of the leg spacing. To measure it, sit in the chair on a dowel laid crosswise under the side rails and find where it balances.
Set to 8 degrees. Dining and office seats slope 2 to 5 degrees, easy chairs more, and 17 degrees has been called extreme.
How far the chair can tip back before the rear tip leaves no rocker under it. This sets the rear overhang.

What the numbers rest on

  • Leg spacing does not set the curve. The radius comes from how high the sitter's center of gravity is. Leg spacing sets the rocker length, where the legs land on it, and the joint heights.
  • Published: the radius must be larger than the center of gravity height. With a center of gravity about 25 to 26 in off the floor, 39 to 42 in is the reasonable range, 30 in is tippy and 80 in barely rocks (Popular Woodworking). Makers report 36 to 48 in overall (Fine Woodworking forum).
  • Published: normal rocking uses only 8 to 10 in of the rocker, and extra length belongs at the rear.
  • Store-bought check: a Van Dyke's adult runner is 36 1/4 in long, 1 1/4 in wide and 1 5/8 in tall with a 4 1/4 in end rise, which works out to about a 41 in radius.
  • My own rules, not published standards: automatic radius is 1.6 times the center of gravity height (it lands in the published range at 25 to 26 in); the 25 degree rock-back reserve; the 5 in front overhang (sized by eye from a porch rocker photo); a 25.5 in center of gravity, on the basis that the cushion lifts the sitter to normal rocker height; the 8 degree seat tilt, picked between the 2 to 5 degree range for upright chairs and the 17 degrees a Woodworker's Journal editor called extreme (no published optimum exists); the balance point default of one third. Change any of them under Settings above.
  • The geometry is exact circle math. It treats the legs as plumb when the chair is at rest. Mock up one rocker in scrap or plywood before cutting good stock.
